Editorial policy & calculator methodology

This page explains who is responsible for CarSales Tracker Pro's content, why the guides exist, how numerical examples are built, where AI assistance fits, and what happens when a reader finds an error.

Purpose: help a salesperson understand their own numbers

The site has one primary audience and one primary job. It serves automotive salespeople who want to understand commission, compare pay plans, keep an independent deal record, and ask better questions before payday. The guides are not written to cover unrelated trending topics, and they are not a replacement for a signed compensation plan or professional advice.

A page should leave a reader able to do something concrete: calculate a deal, identify the lines on a commission statement, understand a draw balance, compare two plan structures, or build a repeatable follow-up habit. If a topic cannot produce a useful answer for that audience, it does not belong here.

Who publishes the site

Michael Dube, creator of CarSales Tracker Pro and founder of Andromeda Kinship, is the named publisher. Apple independently identifies Michael as the app's developer and seller on its public App Store listing. The support address support@andromedakinship.com reaches the operator responsible for the product and website.

How commission examples are modeled

Examples use explicit assumptions so the arithmetic can be checked. Unless a page states otherwise, the basic front-end formula is:

Commissionable gross = max(0, front-end gross − pack)
Percentage commission = commissionable gross × commission rate
Deal commission = the greater of percentage commission or the applicable mini

Back-end participation, base salary, draw recovery, volume bonuses, spiffs, and chargebacks are shown separately when a tool or example includes them. A blended rate may be useful for estimating a month, but it should not be confused with a store's actual front-end and back-end percentages.

The calculators run entirely in the browser. They do not have access to dealership accounting, the user's employer, or a signed pay plan. Their output is an estimate based only on the numbers the user enters.

Ranges are illustrative, not universal

Pay-plan structures are private and vary widely. When a guide describes a commission rate, pack, mini, bonus tier, or income range as “common” or “typical,” it is an orientation point—not a claim that most dealerships use that exact figure. The signed plan always overrides a general example.

For broader labor-market context, the site prefers first-party public sources. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Retail Sales Workers profile, for example, reports wage information for the wider retail-sales occupation and separately identifies automobile dealers as an industry. BLS notes that reported wages include commissions and production bonuses, but its category is broader than dealership floor sales alone. We state that limitation whenever the data is used.

Use of AI and automation

AI-assisted tools may be used for research organization, outlines, first drafts, editing, internal-link suggestions, repetitive markup, and checking repeated calculations. That use is disclosed because readers reasonably want to know how a growing library is produced. AI output is not treated as evidence, and an AI system is never cited as the source for a factual claim.

The useful role of automation here is consistency: running the same formula across examples, spotting a mismatched assumption, checking links, and keeping structured data and navigation aligned. The publisher remains responsible for the final page. Automation does not change the site's purpose from helping car salespeople to attracting search visits.

Sources and external links

When an outside fact can be verified, we link to the original or most authoritative practical source available. Government data should link to the responsible government agency. App ratings and developer identity link to Apple's live listing. A calculator on a related owned site is labeled as an external tool rather than presented as independent research.

External links are chosen because they help the reader complete the task. They are not a guarantee of the outside site's accuracy, and a link does not imply endorsement.

Advertising and commercial interests

CarSales Tracker Pro is both a product and a publisher. Guides may link to the free web tracker, the iOS app, paid subscriptions, or another Andromeda Kinship property when the destination genuinely helps with the page's topic. Those relationships are not hidden. The disclaimer explains advertising and affiliate treatment in more detail.

Advertising is not allowed to interrupt calculator inputs, disguise itself as navigation, or determine what a guide recommends. During site-approval work, empty advertising placements are removed rather than shown as blank boxes.

Updates, corrections, and dates

A page's “updated” date changes only when its substance changes—for example, a calculation is corrected, a source is refreshed, a new section is added, or the product workflow has materially changed. Dates are not advanced merely to make old content look fresh.

To report an error, email support@andromedakinship.com with the page URL and the specific sentence, link, or calculation. Do not send unredacted customer records, pay statements, or confidential dealership documents. Reproducible calculator defects are prioritized because they can affect many readers.

Professional-advice boundary

The guides and calculators provide educational information, not financial, tax, legal, payroll, or employment advice. A reader making a decision about wages, deductions, taxes, or legal rights should consult the relevant written agreement and an appropriate qualified professional.
