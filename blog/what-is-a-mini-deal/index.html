What Is a Mini Deal in Car Sales?

Ask any salesperson about minis and you'll get a groan. A mini deal is the flat minimum commission you earn when a car sells with too little gross to pay a real percentage. Here's how they work — and when taking one is actually the right call.

Published by Michael DubeCreator of CarSales Tracker Pro · methodology & corrections
The short version

A "mini" is a flat floor (often $50–$150) you get instead of a percentage when a car's gross is too low. Minis aren't failures — they're units, and units drive volume bonuses. Model minis in the calculator →

The definition

On most pay plans you earn a percentage of a car's gross profit. But some cars sell at almost no gross — a hard price-shopper, an aged unit the manager wants gone, a tough trade that eats the front. A percentage of $200 is pocket change, so the dealership guarantees a minimum commission instead. That minimum is the mini.

The mechanic is simple: if your percentage would pay less than the mini, you get the mini. Mini amounts vary by store — commonly $50, $100, or $150.

Why minis happen

  • Price competition. Online shopping means customers arrive knowing the market. Thin margins are normal on popular models.
  • Aged inventory. A car sitting 90+ days costs the store money. Managers will give up gross to move it — and you'll mini it.
  • Tough trades. Overallowing on a trade to make a deal work pulls front gross down toward the mini.
  • Volume plays. Near month-end, stores chasing a manufacturer bonus will "give cars away" — minis for you, bonus for them.

When taking a mini is the smart move

New salespeople hate minis and sometimes blow deals trying to avoid them. Don't. A mini is still a unit, and units are what trigger the big money:

  • Volume bonuses. If hitting 15 cars unlocks a bonus across your whole month, the mini that gets you to 15 might be worth far more than its $100 face value.
  • Momentum and ranking. Unit count drives your standing, your ups, and your manager's confidence in you.
  • Referrals and CSI. A happy mini customer still sends you their cousin and still scores you a 10 on the survey.

The pros take the mini, log it, and move to the next up. They fight for gross where it's there and never let a thin deal walk over pride.

How minis change your math

Minis quietly reshape your check. Sell 12 cars but mini five of them, and your average commission per car can drop sharply even if your "big" deals were strong. The only way to see it clearly is to track each deal's real gross and whether it minied.

FAQ

How much is a mini deal worth?
It depends on your dealership, but minis commonly range from $50 to $150 per car. Your pay plan defines the exact amount and the gross threshold below which it applies.
Are minis bad?
Not on their own. A mini is still a unit, and units drive volume bonuses, ranking, and referrals. The mistake is blowing a deal — or your attitude — over avoiding one.
How do I avoid too many minis?
Hold gross where the market allows, present numbers with confidence, and lead with value over price. But track your mini rate first — you can't fix a number you don't measure.
