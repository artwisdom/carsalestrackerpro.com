How to Sell More Cars: 12 Tactics That Move Units

Selling more cars isn't about a magic close. It's a handful of unglamorous habits done relentlessly while everyone else waits for the next up. Here are twelve that consistently separate the top dogs from the pack.

Published by Michael DubeCreator of CarSales Tracker Pro · methodology & corrections
The short version

Work your pipeline, follow up faster and longer than anyone, lead with value over price, and track every lead and deal so nothing slips. Volume plus held gross beats luck every month.

Sell to the people you already met

1. Follow up like your check depends on it — because it does. Most sales happen after multiple contacts, yet most reps quit after one. A simple, persistent cadence (day 1, 3, 7, 14, 30) on every un-sold up will out-produce any closing trick.

2. Own your sold customers. Your past buyers are your easiest future sales and your best referral source. Check in at delivery+1 week, at service visits, and around their payoff/equity sweet spot. A buyer in a positive-equity position is a deal waiting to happen.

3. Mine the orphan owners. Every dealership has customers whose salesperson left. Ask for the list. They already bought from the store — they just need someone to call.

Run a real pipeline, not a memory

4. Track every lead and deal in one place. The single biggest difference between a 9-car rep and an 18-car rep is usually not talent — it's that the 18-car rep never forgets a follow-up, a pending C.O.D., or a customer waiting on a second-key. Memory leaks money.

5. Work your appointments. A set, confirmed appointment closes far better than a walk-in. Set the next step on every contact — a time, not "sometime."

6. Protect your deliveries. A sold deal isn't money until it's delivered and the paperwork is done. Stalled registrations, missing insurance, unfinished trades — these quietly kill units. Stay on them.

Hold gross while you add units

7. Lead with value, not price. When you sell the right car for their needs and budget, price becomes one factor instead of the only one. Reps who only compete on price live in mini-deal land.

8. Present numbers with confidence. Hesitation invites negotiation. Know your figures cold and present them like they're correct — because they are.

9. Get the payment right. Most customers buy a monthly payment, not a price. Knowing how term and rate change the number lets you steer to a car that fits. Run it fast with a free auto loan calculator so you're never guessing in front of a customer.

Build the daily engine

10. Start the day with a plan. Walk in with a call list and appointment goals, not just hope for traffic. The pros are working leads while everyone else is on their phone in the bullpen.

11. Know your numbers. Your units to bonus, your average gross, your mini rate. When you know exactly where you stand, you make smarter end-of-month decisions — like which deal is worth a mini to hit the bonus. (See how commission works and when to take a mini.)

12. Ask for the referral and the review. Every happy customer is two more: a referral and a five-star review that brings the next one. Ask every time.

FAQ

What's the fastest way to sell more cars?
Relentless follow-up on the leads you already have. Most deals close after several contacts, and most reps stop after one — so a simple, tracked follow-up cadence beats almost anything else.
How many cars should a salesperson sell a month?
It varies by store, but a common solid target is 10–15 units. New reps build toward that; top performers push well past it. Track your count so you always know where you stand against your bonus.
How do I hold more gross?
Sell value over price, present numbers with confidence, and avoid over-allowing on trades. Then measure your average gross per car so you can see what's actually working.
