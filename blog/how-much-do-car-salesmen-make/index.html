How Much Do Car Salesmen Make?

There is no single honest “car salesman salary.” Store traffic, pay-plan rules, units, gross, minis, back-end participation, bonuses, and draw treatment can move two salespeople's checks in opposite directions. The useful answer is a calculation you can audit.

Published by Michael DubeCreator of CarSales Tracker Pro · methodology & corrections
The short version

Start with units × average commission per car, then add guaranteed pay and bonuses and subtract recoverable draw or chargebacks. Published “averages” rarely reveal those assumptions. Estimate your own scenario →

The only formula that matters

Strip away the noise and car sales income is just:

Units sold × average commission per car + bonuses − draw owed

Everything else — pack, minis, back-end, spiffs — is detail that rolls up into your average commission per car. Move either number (units or average) and your income moves with it. That's why the best reps obsess over both.

What official wage data can—and cannot—tell you

The closest broad public benchmark is the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics category for retail sales workers. In its May 2024 industry table, BLS reported a $21.00 median hourly wage for retail salespersons working at automobile dealers. BLS also explains that these wage estimates include commissions and production bonuses.

That figure is useful context, but it is not a dealership-salesperson guarantee. The occupation is broader than showroom floor sales, an hourly median can hide large month-to-month swings, and an individual store's draw, bonus tiers, schedule, and commission formula still control the actual check. See the BLS Retail Sales Workers profile for the source and its methodology.

Three worked pay scenarios

The following are models, not claimed national averages. Their value is that every assumption is visible, so you can replace it with your own pay-plan numbers.

The middle row is 12 × $425 + $750 = $5,850. It does not assume that $5,850 is typical; it shows exactly what must be true for that result. If four of those cars become $150 minis, the same month drops by $1,100 before any other adjustment. That sensitivity is why one universal salary number is not very useful.

Lever 1: Units (volume)

More ups, better follow-up, and a full pipeline mean more cars. Volume also unlocks bonuses that can be worth more than the cars that triggered them — which is why pros chase units hard near month-end, even taking minis to get there.

Lever 2: Average per car (gross)

Two reps can both sell 12 cars and take home wildly different checks. The difference is how much gross each holds — value-selling, confident number presentation, and protecting the front from over-allowed trades. A $150 swing in your average across 12 cars is $1,800 a month.

Build your own car-sales pay estimate

Use a recent full month rather than your best month. Pull the following numbers from your own deal log and commission statement:

  1. Delivered units: count only deals your plan treats as commissionable.
  2. Actual commission per deal: include minis at their real payout rather than replacing them with a hoped-for average.
  3. Guaranteed pay: separate a true non-recoverable base from a recoverable draw.
  4. Bonuses and spiffs: include only thresholds you actually reached.
  5. Adjustments: subtract draw recovery and known chargebacks.

Add the deal commissions, guaranteed pay, and earned bonuses, then subtract adjustments. Divide by delivered units to find your real average commission per car. That personal average is usually more useful for planning than a national figure.

Compare the plan, not just the advertised percentage

A store advertising 30% may pay less than a 25% store if the first store applies a large pack, excludes back-end gross, or has difficult bonus cliffs. Conversely, a lower rate can be attractive when there is a non-recoverable salary, low pack, back-end participation, and attainable unit bonuses. Why "average" reps stay average

Most salespeople have no idea what their real average per car is. They remember the big deals and forget the minis, so their mental math is always rosier than payday. FAQ

Can a car salesperson make six figures?
Yes, a pay plan can produce six-figure annual income when unit count, commission per car, and bonuses are high enough. It is not guaranteed. For example, a modeled $8,500 average month equals $102,000 before taxes, while one $8,500 month does not prove a full-year result.
How much does a new car salesperson make?
There is no reliable universal starting figure because stores use different base, draw, mini, and commission rules. Ask for the written plan, model a conservative unit count, and clarify whether any draw is recoverable before accepting an earnings estimate.
Does BLS publish a car-salesman salary?
BLS publishes broader retail-sales occupation data and an automobile-dealer industry figure, not a guarantee for dealership floor salespeople. Its May 2024 table reported a $21.00 median hourly wage for retail salespersons at automobile dealers and notes that wage data include commissions and production bonuses.
