How to Follow Up With Car Sales Leads

Most car deals aren't lost at the close — they're lost in the days after the first contact, when nobody followed up. The reps who consistently hit their numbers aren't always the smoothest talkers; they're the ones with a follow-up system. Here's a practical one: how fast to respond, a day-by-day cadence, which channels to use, what to say, and how to make sure no lead ever slips through the cracks.

Published by Michael DubeCreator of CarSales Tracker Pro · methodology & corrections
The short version

Respond in minutes, not hours. Work a planned cadence of calls, texts and emails over the first two weeks, then a longer drip. Lead with value, not "just checking in." And track every lead so none gets forgotten — then know what each one is worth.

1. Speed to lead: the first few minutes win

The single biggest follow-up edge is simply being first and being fast. An internet lead you answer in five minutes is dramatically warmer than the same lead called back two hours later — by then they've filled out three other forms and someone else has them on the phone. Treat a fresh lead like a customer standing on your lot: stop what you're doing and respond. If you can't call immediately, fire a quick text to plant your flag, then call.

2. Build a follow-up cadence

A cadence is just a planned schedule of touches so you're not guessing when to reach out. A simple, proven shape for a car lead looks like this:

WhenTouch
Minute 0–5Call + text the instant the lead lands
Day 1 (later)Second call at a different time of day
Day 2Value text (a specific unit, a number, an answer)
Day 4Email with options + a clear next step
Day 7Call — check what changed
Day 14"Still looking?" text
Day 30+Move to a monthly long-term drip

The exact days matter less than having a schedule and working it. Most reps quit after one or two attempts; the deals are sitting in touches three through seven.

3. Mix your channels

Don't hammer one channel. People answer differently:

  • Call: still the highest-intent touch. Vary the time of day — someone who won't pick up at 10am may answer at 6pm.
  • Text: the highest response rate for quick questions. Keep it short, specific, and signed with your name and store.
  • Email: best for options, photos, and out-the-door numbers they can sit with. Always end with one clear next step.

4. What to actually say

"Just checking in" gives the customer nothing to respond to. Lead with a reason. A few that work:

  • Specific unit: "Hi Maria, it's Sam at [store]. The silver CR-V you asked about is still here and I set it aside — want to grab it tonight or tomorrow?"
  • New information: "Quick one — a rebate just changed on that trim. Worth two minutes on the phone. Good to call now?"
  • Easy yes/no: "Still in the market, or did you already find something?" (Even a "found something" frees you to move on.)

Match your tone to the channel, always identify yourself and the store, and give them one simple action to take.

5. Don't quit at the first no

A "no" is usually "not right now." Most of the customers who eventually buy needed several touches first, and a polite, useful rep who's still around in two weeks often wins the deal the pushy one walked away from. Persistence with value — not pestering — is the whole game.

6. Track every lead — or the system falls apart

A cadence only works if you remember who's on it. The fastest way to lose a deal is to lose the lead in a notebook, a text thread, and your memory all at once. FAQ

How many times should you follow up with a car lead?
Plan for at least five to seven touches over the first two weeks, then move uncontacted leads to a longer monthly drip. Most reps stop after one or two attempts — which is exactly where the deals are left.
How fast should you respond to an internet car lead?
As close to immediately as possible. A lead answered within a few minutes is far warmer than one returned hours later, because online shoppers contact several dealers at once. Speed is the cheapest advantage you have.
Should I call, text, or email a lead?
Use all three. Calls carry the most intent, texts get the fastest replies for quick questions, and email is best for options and numbers. Vary the time of day and always give one clear next step.
