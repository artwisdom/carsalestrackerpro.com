Your First 30 Days in Car Sales

The first month on the floor is a firehose: product knowledge, the CRM, the desk, the process, and a hundred new faces. Most green peas survive it on adrenaline. Here's how to come out of it with momentum instead of just relief.

The short version

Week 1: learn product and process. Week 2: get reps with real ups. Week 3: build a follow-up system. Week 4: protect your deliveries and ask for referrals. Track every up from day one so month two starts with a pipeline, not a blank slate.

Week 1 — Learn the product and the process

You can't sell what you can't explain. Spend week one learning your top-selling models cold: trims, key features, what makes each better for a specific buyer. Walk the lot daily so you know your inventory by heart.

Equally important: learn the process — the steps from greeting to delivery, who does what at the desk, and how a deal flows. Knowing the road to the sale keeps you calm when a real up walks in.

Week 2 — Get reps, embrace the awkward

Take every up you can. You'll fumble. That's the job in week two. The goal isn't to close everything — it's to get comfortable doing a walk-around, a demo drive, and a number presentation without reading from a script. Shadow a top rep and steal their language.

Start a simple habit now: write down every customer — name, vehicle, where you left it, and the next step. This one habit is what separates reps who build a pipeline from reps who start from zero every morning.

Week 3 — Build your follow-up system

By now you have un-sold ups piling up. Most rookies let them die. You won't. Set a contact cadence — day 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 — and actually work it. The deals you "lost" in week one are still buyers; they just haven't been called by anyone persistent. Your follow-up is your first real pipeline.

Week 4 — Protect deliveries, ask for referrals

Closing a deal is only half the job. In week four, learn to protect your deliveries: confirm insurance, chase registrations, finish trades, collect any C.O.D. A sold deal that doesn't deliver isn't a unit — and rookies lose more than they think to stalled paperwork.

Then, from your very first delivery, ask for the referral and the review. Early reps skip this and leave their easiest future units on the table.

What to ignore in month one

  • Bullpen gossip and doom-talk. Every store has reps who explain why nobody can sell. Don't catch it.
  • Comparing your check to veterans'. You're building skills and a customer base. The money follows.
  • Fancy closes you saw online. Fundamentals — greet, qualify, demo, present, follow up — out-sell tricks every time.

FAQ

Is the first month in car sales hard?
It's a steep learning curve — product, process, and people all at once — but it's very survivable with a plan. Focus on fundamentals and start tracking your customers immediately.
How many cars will I sell my first month?
Many new reps sell a handful (often 4–8) while they learn. Don't judge yourself on month one; judge yourself on whether you built habits that make month two better.
What's the most important rookie habit?
Writing down every customer and following up. The reps who track their ups from day one build a pipeline; the ones who rely on memory start over every morning.
