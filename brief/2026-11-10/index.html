FTC Settlement Puts Dealer Price Ads Under Review

Dealer Ops Daily · Tuesday, November 10, 2026 · 5-minute read

Running the morning meeting? Save card

The FTC just proved it's still checking whether your advertised price matches what customers actually pay — and a second case shows debt collectors using fake balances to squeeze buyers after the sale. Both are reminders that what happens after the signature still gets watched.

⚖️ Protect the deal

FTC Settles With Dealer Group Over Mismatched Price Ads

The FTC announced a settlement with Stream Auto Outlet, a Long Island dealer group, resolving claims it advertised prices far below what buyers actually paid, according to the FTC's Nov. 9 release. The agency says the gap violated its Motor Vehicle Dealers Trade Regulation Rule on price transparency.

Use it today: Before you post or quote a deal, read your own ad back line by line — make sure the walk-in price matches the number you put on the desk.

Sources: FTC Press Releases

Court Halts Debt Collectors Using Fake Balances

A federal court temporarily froze the assets of a debt collection operation accused of using false threats to collect more than $20 million in often fictitious debts, per the FTC's complaint. The allegations are not yet adjudicated.

Use it today: If a customer mentions a collector calling about a car loan that doesn't match what's in the DMS, loop in your F&I manager before you move the deal forward.

Sources: FTC Press Releases

💰 The desk & the box

Synthetic Identities Now Drive Most Auto Finance Fraud

Synthetic identities now account for the majority of vehicle-financing fraud, according to F&I and Showroom's Nov. 9 report; the claim has not been independently verified by other outlets. These files blend real and fake data to pass initial credit checks and often surface only after a loan funds and payments stop.

Use it today: Flag any file with a credit history under 12 months paired with a trade you've never seen — ask the desk to run a secondary verification before it goes to funding.

Sources: F&I and Showroom (FI Magazine)

🚗 Market numbers

GM's $750M Hybrid Push Lines Up With A Bigger Forecast

GM is putting $750 million into expanding its hybrid lineup because it can't keep sitting out the segment, Automotive News reported Nov. 9. A separate joint industry report, covered by AutoSuccess, projects hybrids will reach 32% of U.S. new-vehicle sales by 2035 as automakers add lower-priced options.

Use it today: If you're carrying hybrid inventory, stop treating it as a side note — ask your desk for the hybrid-specific talking points before your next up walks in.

Sources: Automotive News · AutoSuccess

🏬 The floor

97% Of Dealer Websites Fail Google's Speed Test

97.1% of dealership websites fail Google's Core Web Vitals test on mobile or desktop, according to an audit cited by Digital Dealer. Mobile interactivity failures hit 88% of sites, with a median response time of 313 milliseconds against Google's 200-millisecond bar — a gap the audit ties to $30 of every $100 in digital ad spend going to waste.

Use it today: Ask your internet manager if your store's site has been tested on a phone this month — a slow vehicle page is a lost up before the call ever comes in.

Sources: Digital Dealer / Dealer Magazine
