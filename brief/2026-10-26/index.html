Congress's Kill-Switch Law, Decoded for the Floor

Dealer Ops Daily · Monday, October 26, 2026 · 5-minute read

Running the morning meeting? Save card

Congress clarified what the new 'kill-switch' law actually requires — and it's narrower than the rumors flying around your showroom. Meanwhile, Q3 financing data shows record loan amounts and more trade-ins arriving underwater than usual.

⚖️ Protect the deal

Congress's 'Kill-Switch' Law Isn't What the Rumors Say

Congress has moved forward on a federal mandate requiring new vehicles to include technology that can detect and prevent impaired driving, according to CBT News (Oct. 23, 2026); the law is not yet independently verified beyond that reporting. It's not a remote government shutoff — it's an impairment-detection system tied to future model years. NHTSA has not yet published a compliance timeline.

Use it today: If an up repeats the 'government can kill my car' rumor, correct it: the law targets impaired-driving prevention in future models, not remote control of what's already on your lot. Point skeptical buyers to NHTSA's own site, not social media.

Sources: CBT News

NHTSA Updates Lighting Rules for Autonomous Vehicles

NHTSA finalized updates to vehicle lighting standards specifically for autonomous vehicles, per the agency's own release (Oct. 23, 2026). The agency frames the change as a technical clean-up rather than a new mandate on dealers or current inventory. No new disclosure requirement applies to today's deals.

Use it today: If a customer asks whether self-driving tech is 'still getting regulated,' tell them yes — NHTSA just updated AV lighting rules this week. It's proof the oversight is ongoing, not absent.

Sources: NHTSA

💰 The desk & the box

Q3 Financing Hit Record Highs — and So Did Negative Equity

New-vehicle financing amounts reached record levels in the third quarter while negative equity kept climbing, according to Auto Dealer Today's Q3 Dealer Debrief (Oct. 23, 2026); exact figures weren't published, so treat this as directional. The pattern: more trade-ins are arriving underwater than in recent quarters.

Use it today: Pull the payoff quote before you structure the deal, not after. If the trade's upside down, show the customer the math on rolling the difference early — don't let the desk discover it at the four-square.

Sources: Auto Dealer Today

🏬 The floor

A Ford Store Is Turning Mobile Service Into a Loyalty Engine

Bozard Ford built out a mobile-service program that goes beyond oil changes and recalls, driving repeat business and retention, according to CBT News (Oct. 23, 2026). The store treats mobile service as a standing offer, not a one-off perk.

Use it today: Ask your service drive manager if mobile service is being pitched on every delivery. If it's recall-only right now, that's a retention opportunity your floor isn't using to bring customers back for their next trade.

Sources: CBT News

Unifor Clears the Way for a Possible Stellantis Strike in Canada

Unifor members authorized a potential strike at Stellantis' Canadian plants, according to CBT News (Oct. 23, 2026); no strike date has been set. A work stoppage would hit Jeep, Ram, Dodge, and Chrysler production lines that feed U.S. stores.

Use it today: If you're moving Jeep, Ram, Dodge, or Chrysler inventory, start asking your inventory manager about incoming allocation now — a strike could tighten supply on those lines within weeks.

Sources: CBT News
