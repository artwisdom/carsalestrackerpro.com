The Week Affordability, Consolidation, and Pricing Rules Converged

Dealer Ops Daily · Sunday, October 18, 2026 · 5-minute read

Running the morning meeting? Save card

Three threads ran through all five issues this week, and they're connected: new-car prices parked at a record, the groups buying stores kept buying, and the FTC put a price tag on advertised numbers that don't hold up. None of it changes who walks in Monday. All of it changes what you say to them.

• Top-Stories

New-vehicle prices hit $50,562 — and buyers are trading down to used

The average new-vehicle transaction price reached $50,562 in September, just $52 off December's record, according to CBT News (Oct. 15), with the same data showing buyers shifting toward used inventory. Kelley Blue Book analysis reported by F&I and Showroom (Oct. 14) found new prices essentially flat month over month — no relief, no cliff. Meanwhile CBT News (Oct. 14) reported average monthly payments and 84-month terms both at new highs, and F&I and Showroom reported last month's rate increase with a possible second hike adding to the squeeze.

Use it today: Why it matters: The 'I'll wait for prices to drop' objection has no data behind it this quarter — flat prices plus climbing rates means waiting costs money, and you can say that with a source.

Sources: CBT News · F&I and Showroom (citing Kelley Blue Book) · CBT News · F&I and Showroom

Penske goes private at $16.3B as buy/sell deals hit a record 462

Mitsui & Co. and Penske Corp. are buying out the remaining public shares of Penske Automotive in a deal valued at $16.3 billion, split roughly 64-36, according to Wards Auto (Oct. 15), which noted the transaction had not yet been independently confirmed elsewhere. That landed in the same week AutoSuccess (Oct. 13) reported a record 462 completed U.S. dealership buy/sell transactions over the past year, and CBT News (Oct. 12) reported O'Brien Auto Team's purchase of Jack Kain Ford in Kentucky. The pattern that emerged: ownership is changing hands at every tier at once, from single-point family stores to the largest public groups.

Use it today: Why it matters: Ownership changes are when pay plans get rewritten — this week's theme in one sentence, and the reason to get your comp terms in writing before the sign changes.

Sources: Wards Auto · AutoSuccess · CBT News

FTC settles with a dealer group over advertised prices that weren't the price

Greenway Automotive agreed to a proposed FTC settlement over allegations its advertised prices weren't the real price, according to Automotive News (Oct. 9). Separately, Digital Dealer (Oct. 9) reports federal guidance and California rules are pushing dealers toward a single all-in price everywhere a vehicle is listed. The quieter companion story: a Car Dealership Guy News analysis (Oct. 14, not independently verified) found a number of Carvana Google Business Profile listings pointing to wrong addresses — a reminder that what's published about your store is its own exposure.

Use it today: Why it matters: Advertised-price accuracy moved from a marketing detail to a documented enforcement risk this week, and the gap is usually found on third-party listings nobody on the floor audits.

Sources: Automotive News (via ASOTU) · Digital Dealer

• Data-Recap

The week in numbers

$50,562 — September's average new-vehicle transaction price, $52 under December's record, per CBT News (Oct. 15). The number itself is the objection; have a used comparable ready before it comes up.

462 — record dealership buy/sell transactions in the past year, per AutoSuccess (Oct. 13). Consolidation is the backdrop to every pay-plan conversation you'll have this quarter.

$16.3B — the Penske Automotive go-private valuation, per Wards Auto (Oct. 15). When the largest groups restructure, store-level benchmarking usually follows.

-12% — Stellantis Q3 North American shipments, against a 1% global decline, per CBT News (Oct. 13). Jeep and Ram floors should confirm incoming allocation before promising a trim.

106,000 — model-year 2024-2027 Land Cruisers recalled over a fuel tube leak risk, per Toyota's own recall notice (Oct. 14). Run VINs on inventory and incoming trades before you show them.

Use it today: Pick the two numbers that apply to your franchise and put them in your phone notes — cited figures beat opinions on a Monday up.

Sources: CBT News · AutoSuccess · Wards Auto · CBT News · Toyota USA Newsroom

• Watch-Next-Week

What to watch next week

Rate direction. F&I and Showroom reported last month's increase and flagged the possibility of a second hike this month; if it lands, every payment conversation on your floor resets. Next week's theme here is subprime and captive lenders, and this is the setup for it.

The next Manheim read. Wards/Automotive Dive (Oct. 8) reported Q3 wholesale depreciation accelerating while high-MPG units held value on fuel costs. Watch whether that MPG split widens — it's the appraisal variable right now.

All-in pricing follow-through. After the proposed Greenway settlement reported by Automotive News (Oct. 9) and the federal and California rules covered by Digital Dealer, watch for the next enforcement action or guidance update. Also open: the Porsche franchise allocation suit reported by CBT News (Oct. 13), which tests how much control OEMs hold over what reaches your lot.

Use it today: Block fifteen minutes Monday to audit your store's listings and your own Land Cruiser VINs — both are faster to fix before a customer finds them.

Sources: F&I and Showroom · Wards/Automotive Dive (citing Manheim Used Vehicle Value Index) · Automotive News (via ASOTU) · CBT News

• Tool-Of-Week

Tool of the week: NHTSA's public complaint and recall database (free, no affiliate)

No sponsored pick this week — the most useful tool that came across the desk was free. Digital Dealer (Oct. 15) outlined how used-car departments can pull NHTSA's public owner-complaint and recall records by year, make and model, a layer that condition reports and vehicle history reports routinely miss. With 106,000 Land Cruisers recalled this week per Toyota's notice and appraisal values splitting by fuel economy, that lookup is a two-minute step with real recon and disclosure consequences.

Use it today: Bookmark nhtsa.gov on your phone and run the model year before you pencil your next trade number — flag anything that changes recon cost or needs disclosing.

Sources: Digital Dealer / Dealer Magazine · NHTSA.gov (public complaint database)
