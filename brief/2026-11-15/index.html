The Week Your Advertised Price Became the Story

Dealer Ops Daily · Sunday, November 15, 2026 · 5-minute read

Three threads ran through all five issues this week: regulators kept circling advertised prices, AI kept getting adopted faster than it got governed, and the average new vehicle got more expensive again. None of those are headlines you act on once. They're conditions you sell inside of starting Monday.

• Top-Stories

The FTC Spent the Week on Price Transparency

On Nov. 9 the FTC announced a settlement with Long Island's Stream Auto Outlet, resolving claims it advertised prices far below what buyers actually paid under the Motor Vehicle Dealers Trade Regulation Rule, according to the FTC's release. By Nov. 11, Automotive News and CBT News reported rival dealer groups rewriting ad language to match the numbers on the desk. Then on Nov. 12 the FTC and 17 states announced a $63 million proposed settlement with Family Dollar over register-versus-shelf price mismatches — a proposed settlement subject to court approval, not a fine, according to the FTC announcement. Whether any given pricing discrepancy violates the law depends on the specific facts and applicable law.

Use it today: Pull three of your store's live listings and read them against the window sticker before Monday's first up. If the walk-in number doesn't match, flag it to your desk yourself.

Sources: FTC Press Releases · FTC Press Releases · CBT News

Stores Adopted AI Faster Than They Wrote Rules For It

The pattern that emerged across three separate reports: adoption is ahead of governance. A CDK survey covered by Auto Dealer Today on Nov. 6 found most dealerships now use AI somewhere in operations while few have built guardrails against AI-enabled attacks. AutoSuccess reported Nov. 12 that 57% of dealership staff already use AI tools day to day, with most stores lacking rules on which tools may touch customer data. Digital Dealer reported Nov. 12 that generative tools can now fabricate convincing pay stubs and licenses, eroding traditional income checks; that claim has not been independently verified.

Use it today: Before you paste a customer's name, income, or SSN into any AI tool this week, ask your GM or IT lead whether there's an approved-tools list. No answer is itself the answer.

Sources: Auto Dealer Today · AutoSuccess · Digital Dealer / Dealer Magazine

A Record $51,109 Average Pushed Shoppers Toward CPO

The average new-vehicle transaction price hit $51,109 in October, up 1.3% from September and 2.1% year over year, according to Kelley Blue Book data reported by Digital Dealer on Nov. 12. F&I and Showroom reported Nov. 11 that the climb is partly mix — October sales skewed toward bigger, pricier models — while budget-conscious buyers moved down-trim. CBT News reported Nov. 12 that dealers are leaning harder on certified pre-owned as affordability pressure builds. Separately, JM&A Group's Q3 report, covered by CBT News, showed F&I PVR and product penetration ahead of last year while front-end PVR softened.

Use it today: When a new-car up flinches at the payment, walk them to the CPO row the same day — and present the full menu there, because that's where the gross is actually sitting this quarter.

Sources: Digital Dealer / Dealer Magazine · F&I and Showroom (FI Magazine) · CBT News · CBT News

• Data-Recap

The Week in Numbers

• $51,109 — October's average new-vehicle transaction price, a record, up 2.1% year over year (Kelley Blue Book, via Digital Dealer). Say the number out loud to price-shocked ups: the whole market moved, not your sticker.

• 57% — share of dealership staff using AI tools day to day (AutoSuccess, Nov. 12). More than half your floor is already doing this, with or without a policy.

• 97.1% — dealership websites failing Google's Core Web Vitals on mobile or desktop, with a median mobile response of 313 milliseconds against a 200-millisecond bar (audit cited by Digital Dealer, Nov. 10). A slow VDP loses the up before the phone rings.

• 35 — franchises that changed hands in a single week, including five Chevrolet stores (Automotive News, Nov. 6; not independently verified). Consolidation is still running hot, and new rooftops open desk seats before they post them.

• $40–50 — estimated daily cost of a car stuck in recon limbo, per iPacket's Josh Levine speaking to ASOTU (Nov. 12; not independently verified). Two or three idle days per unit is real money off the back end.

Use it today: Pick one of these five to bring to Monday's huddle — the $51,109 figure is the easiest to use on the floor the same morning.

Sources: Digital Dealer / Dealer Magazine · AutoSuccess · Digital Dealer / Dealer Magazine · Automotive News · ASOTU YouTube

• Watch-Next-Week

What to Watch Next Week

• Open FTC thread: the Stream Auto Outlet matter was announced Nov. 9 under the Motor Vehicle Dealers Trade Regulation Rule, and the $63 million Family Dollar price-transparency settlement announced Nov. 12 is a proposed settlement still subject to court approval, according to the FTC. Watch ftc.gov press releases for whether the pricing cases keep coming.

• Stellantis allocation and incentives: Tim Kuniskis took over U.S. sales and dealer networks, according to Automotive News (Nov. 12; not independently verified elsewhere). If you're on a Stellantis point, the first programs under the new structure are the thing to ask your GM about.

• Open recall campaigns in your trade lane: Toyota's Nov. 12 notice covers roughly 314,000 U.S. vehicles with Takata front passenger inflators. Eligibility is by vehicle, not by model year — verify each unit individually.

Use it today: Check any Toyota or Lexus trade by VIN or license plate at Toyota.com/recall or NHTSA.gov/recalls before you commit to an appraisal number.

Sources: FTC Press Releases · Toyota USA Newsroom · NHTSA recall lookup · Automotive News (via Google News)
