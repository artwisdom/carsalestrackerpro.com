FTC Pricing Crackdown Spreads As Record Prices Land

Dealer Ops Daily · Thursday, November 12, 2026 · 5-minute read

Running the morning meeting? Save card

New-vehicle prices just set a record, and the FTC's latest settlement is forcing rivals to match their ads to the sticker before a customer does the math first.

⚖️ Protect the deal

FTC's Stream Auto Settlement Pushes Rivals To Clean Up Ads

The FTC finalized an agreement with Stream Auto Outlet over deceptive vehicle pricing. Automotive News reports other dealer groups are now scrambling to match ad language to actual numbers, with one retail exec telling the outlet rivals still need more 'hand slapping' before ad practices fully align with the rule.

Use it today: Pull your store's current ad pricing against the window sticker before your first up walks in today — if there's a gap, flag it to your desk now, not after a customer catches it.

Sources: CBT News · Automotive News

💰 The desk & the box

F&I PVR Climbed In Q3 Even As Front-End Profit Slipped

According to JM&A Group's Q3 report, as covered by CBT News, F&I PVR and product penetration finished ahead of last year even as front-end vehicle PVR weakened. The split shows F&I carrying more of the gross on each deal than it did a year ago.

Use it today: If your front gross is thin this month, lean into your menu presentation instead of chasing a bigger spread on the vehicle — that's where the store's profit is actually shifting.

Sources: CBT News

🚗 Market numbers

New-Vehicle Prices Hit A Record $51,109 In October

The average transaction price for a new vehicle reached $51,109 in October, up 1.3% from September and 2.1% year over year, according to Kelley Blue Book, a Cox Automotive brand.

Use it today: Use the $51,109 figure directly with price-shocked ups: the whole market moved up, not just your store's sticker.

Sources: Digital Dealer / Dealer Magazine

A Car Stuck In Recon Limbo Costs About $40-50 A Day

According to Josh Levine of iPacket, speaking ahead of an upcoming webinar, cars often sit two to three days in recon because departments don't talk to each other — a finished wheel-and-tire job can sit in the box with nobody told. He says that silence costs roughly $40 to $50 a day per car; not independently verified.

Use it today: Ask your desk how old your oldest recon job is right now — if nobody has an answer in under ten seconds, that's the leak Levine is describing.

Sources: ASOTU YouTube

🏬 The floor

Most Dealership Staff Use AI Now, Few Have A Plan For It

According to AutoSuccess, 57% of dealership staff are already using AI tools day to day, but most stores haven't set rules for which tools can touch customer data or deal structure. The gap between adoption and governance is where variable-ops mistakes start.

Use it today: Before you paste a customer's info into any AI tool today, ask your manager if there's an approved list — if the answer is 'I don't know,' raise it at the next meeting.

Sources: AutoSuccess
