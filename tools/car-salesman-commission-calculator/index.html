How car salesman commission actually works

Almost every dealership pays salespeople a cut of gross profit — the difference between what the customer pays for the car and what the dealership has in it. The most common front-end rate is 20–30% of that gross. Sell a car with $2,000 of front gross on a 25% plan and you've earned $500 on that unit.

Three things complicate the simple math, and this calculator handles all of them:

  • Pack. Many stores subtract a fixed "pack" (often $300–$800) from the gross before calculating your commission. A $2,000 deal with an $800 pack only pays you on $1,200.
  • Mini deals. When a car sells at little or no profit, the percentage would pay you almost nothing — so the store pays a flat mini instead, usually $50–$150. Turn on the mini toggle to model deals that fall below your threshold.
  • Back-end (F&I) gross. Some pay plans give you a slice of the finance-and-insurance profit too. Add your average back-end gross per car and set your blended rate.

On top of the per-car math, most plans layer on volume bonuses (hit 15 units, earn an extra $X each) and spiffs (cash for aging units or specific models). Those vary too much to calculate generically — but they're exactly the kind of thing worth tracking deal by deal.

Frequently asked questions

How is car salesman commission calculated?
Most dealerships pay a percentage — often 20–30% — of the gross profit on each car, sometimes after subtracting a fixed pack. Low-profit deals pay a flat "mini" instead. Your monthly commission is the sum across every car you sold, plus any base salary, spiffs, or volume bonuses.
What is a typical car salesman commission rate?
Front-end commission is commonly 20–30% of gross profit. Many stores also pay a smaller percentage of back-end (F&I) gross. Your exact rate is set by your dealership's pay plan — check it line by line.
What is a mini deal?
A "mini" is the flat minimum commission you earn when a car's gross is too low to beat the percentage — often $50 to $150. If you sell at little or no front gross, you get the mini instead of a percentage.
Do car salesmen get a base salary?
Some do. Many are paid pure commission with a small "draw" (an advance against future commission); others get a modest base plus commission. Volume bonuses for hitting unit targets usually sit on top of either structure.
