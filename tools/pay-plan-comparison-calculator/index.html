How the pay-plan comparison works

The calculator splits your expected month into regular-gross deals and low-gross deals. For regular deals, it subtracts each plan's pack from the average front gross, floors the result at zero, and applies that plan's front-end percentage. For low-gross deals, it runs the same calculation and then uses whichever is larger: the percentage result or the plan's mini. Back-end participation is calculated separately on every unit.

Finally, the tool adds a non-recoverable base or guarantee and any monthly bonus you reasonably expect to earn. It does not assume that a bonus will fire just because the plan contains one. If you only hit a volume tier occasionally, compare the plans once with the bonus and once without it.

Do not enter a recoverable draw as extra pay

A recoverable draw smooths the timing of your check, but it does not increase total compensation. If a store advances $1,500 and later subtracts that $1,500 from earned commission, adding it as base pay would count the same money twice. Only enter an amount in the base field when the plan describes a non-recoverable salary, guarantee, or floor that remains yours under the scenario you are modeling.

If you are unsure which kind of draw is in front of you, read recoverable versus non-recoverable draw before comparing the plans.

Why the lower rate can win

Suppose Plan A pays 30% after a $1,000 pack, while Plan B pays 25% after a $400 pack. On a $2,000 front-gross deal, Plan A pays 30% of $1,000, or $300. Plan B pays 25% of $1,600, or $400. The plan with the smaller percentage is already $100 ahead before back-end participation or bonuses enter the picture.

That does not make a low pack automatically better. One plan may offer a stronger mini, a meaningful monthly guarantee, retroactive unit bonuses, or a back-end split. The point is to compare the whole system at the volume and gross you can actually produce.

Questions to ask before choosing

  • Is pack fixed, and does it differ between new and used inventory?
  • Is the advertised percentage calculated before or after pack?
  • Does back-end participation apply to all F&I gross or only certain products?
  • Is the draw recoverable, and can a negative balance carry into the next month?
  • Are volume bonuses retroactive to every unit or paid only on units above the threshold?
  • What triggers chargebacks, and how long can the dealership claw them back?
  • How many people at the store actually hit the bonus tier in a typical month?

Frequently asked questions

Why can a lower commission rate pay more?
Because pack, minis, back-end participation, base pay, and bonuses can outweigh the headline rate. A 25% plan with a low pack can pay more than a 30% plan with a high pack.
How does this calculator treat mini deals?
For the low-gross units you enter, it calculates front-end percentage after pack and uses the larger of that amount or the plan's mini. Back-end participation is added separately.
Should I enter a recoverable draw as base pay?
Usually no. A recoverable draw is an advance against commission, not extra compensation. Enter only a non-recoverable monthly base or guarantee that remains yours in addition to the modeled commission.
Does the result replace my written pay plan?
No. The comparison is an estimate based on the values you enter. Your signed plan and the dealership's official accounting determine actual compensation.
